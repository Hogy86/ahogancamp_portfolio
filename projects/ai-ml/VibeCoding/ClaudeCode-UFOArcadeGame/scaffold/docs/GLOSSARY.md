# Glossary

**A reference of key terms used throughout the project documentation, code, and gameplay.**

| Term | Definition | Source Doc(s) |
|---|---|---|
| **Vanguard** | The player character — an original humanoid shield-throwing hero (head, arms, torso, legs in blue and white) at the bottom of the playfield. Controllable via arrow keys. Redesigned in v2 per F13. | PRD §F1, §F9, §F13; ADR-0004 |
| **Sentinel** | An enemy robot, humanoid in shape (head, arms, torso, legs) with red eyes, firing red lasers. Sentinels spawn in a formation, descend toward the player, and fire lasers. Regular Sentinels have HP ranging from 1–4 hits depending on level, encoded in body color (white=1-hit, progressively darker gray for 2-4 hits). Bosses are darkest but contrasted against background. Redesigned in v2 per F17. | PRD §F3, §F4, §F17; ADR-0004 |
| **run** | One full playthrough attempt from level 1. A run ends when the player wins (clears level 10 and defeats the level-10 boss) or loses (3 lives exhausted or formation reaches the player's row). Restarting the game via the pause menu starts a new run. | PRD §Summary, terminology note |
| **level** | One stage of play consisting of a single enemy formation to clear (and, on levels 5 and 10, a subsequent boss phase). The game has exactly 10 levels. Clearing all enemies in a level's formation advances to the next level or (on levels 5 and 10) triggers the boss phase. Defeating the level-10 boss triggers the Game Complete celebration and end-of-game state. | PRD §F5, §F12; architecture component responsibilities |
| **formation** | The block of enemy robots arranged in a grid (rows × cols). The formation moves as a rigid unit left/right, steps down when hitting a screen edge, and advances toward the player over time. On levels with a boss phase, the regular formation clears first, then the boss appears. | PRD §F3, §F12; architecture component responsibilities |
| **boss phase** | (v2) The post-formation-clear single-boss encounter on levels 5 and 10 only. The boss phase is part of the same level — it does not increment the level counter, does not trigger a "LEVEL [N]" intro, and does not reset the permanent multiplier. Preceded by a ~1.75-second "BOSS INCOMING" warning cue (F12 AC10). See `bossPhase` type. | PRD §F12, §F18 AC7; `src/core/types.ts` BossPhase |
| **boss-incoming warning** | (v2) A lightweight, ~1.75-second non-freezing telegraph (screen-edge flash and/or "BOSS INCOMING" text) that plays after the regular formation is cleared on a boss level (5 or 10) and before the boss spawns. Unlike the F18 level-intro, the player retains full movement and throw control during the warning. | PRD §F12 AC10-11 |
| **hitsToKill** | The number of shield hits required to destroy a single enemy. Ranges from 1 to 4 for regular enemies; bosses have higher values (15 on level 5, 20 on level 10). Also called "HP" colloquially. | PRD §F4, §F12 AC2; `src/core/types.ts` Enemy interface |
| **boss** | A unique, tougher enemy that appears **only on levels 5 and 10** (not levels 1-4, 6-9) after the regular formation is fully cleared. Bosses have **5× the hit points of the toughest regular enemy on that level** (F12 AC2: level 5 boss = 15 hits, level 10 boss = 20 hits), are **5× the linear size** of a regular enemy (F12 AC5), and are rendered in a boss-unique dark color (F12 AC6, F17 AC5) that clearly contrasts against the black background. Redesigned from v1 to appear post-clear (not embedded in formation) per F12. | PRD §F12, §F4 (v1); ADR-0004 |
| **shield** (projectile) | The projectile thrown by the player when pressing space. Rendered as a **circle in the avatar's blue color (F14)**. Travels and bounces, applying exactly one hit of damage per enemy contact. **One shield may be in flight at a time** (F16 AC3) — the next throw is gated by the current shield leaving play. The shield **bounces off enemies** at deterministic angles based on contact zone (F15 AC1-8), may **bounce multiple times**, and can **return to the player for a +1 life catch** (F16 AC2). Redesigned in v2 per F14 (circular, blue) and F15/F16 (bouncing, one-in-flight, catch mechanic). | PRD §F2, §F14, §F15, §F16; `src/core/types.ts` ShieldProjectile |
| **shield bounce zone** | (v2) One of eight regions of an enemy hitbox perimeter used to classify shield impact and determine bounce direction (F15 AC1-8). Zones are: four corner zones (bounce diagonally), two horizontal side zones (bounce purely horizontal), and two vertical center zones (stop/no bounce). The boundary between corner and side is defined by `SHIELD_CORNER_ZONE_FRACTION` (default 30% of each face width/height). Deterministic and consistent per F15 AC7. | PRD §F15 AC7; `src/config/constants.ts`, `src/systems/CollisionSystem.ts` |
| **power-up** | A collectible drop that falls from a destroyed enemy. The player must catch (collide with) it to activate its effect. Four types exist: HIT_POWER, SPEED, SHIELD (temporary), and PERMANENT_MULTIPLIER. **In v2, the three temporary types share a single active-effect slot** (F11 AC1): at most one is active; catching any temporary power-up cancels the current one immediately. | PRD §F7, §F11 |
| **active-effect slot** | (v2) The single dynamic slot holding **at most one temporary power-up effect at any time** (type + remaining time). Temporary power-ups (5× Hit Power, 3× Speed, Indestructible Shield) share this slot; catching any temporary power-up (same type or different) immediately overwrites it with the new effect at a full fresh 8-second timer (F11 AC1-4). Replaces v1's three independent parallel timers. Encoded in `world.effects: ActiveEffect`. | PRD §F11; `src/core/types.ts` ActiveEffect |
| **HIT_POWER** | A temporary power-up type: while active (8 seconds), each shield hit deals 5× damage. Effect uses the single active-effect slot (F11); catching a new temporary power-up while HIT_POWER is active immediately cancels it. Composes multiplicatively with the permanent multiplier. | PRD §F7 AC4, §F11; `src/core/types.ts` TemporaryEffectType |
| **SPEED** | A temporary power-up type: while active (8 seconds), the player's horizontal movement speed is 3× base. Effect uses the single active-effect slot (F11); catching a new temporary power-up while SPEED is active immediately cancels it. | PRD §F7 AC5, §F11; `src/core/types.ts` TemporaryEffectType |
| **SHIELD** | A temporary power-up type: while active (8 seconds), the player is invulnerable to enemy lasers (no life loss on hit). Effect uses the single active-effect slot (F11); catching a new temporary power-up while SHIELD is active immediately cancels it. Also called "Indestructible Shield" in some contexts. Visually distinguished non-color-only per NFR-9a. | PRD §F7 AC6, §F11; NFR-9(a) |
| **PERMANENT_MULTIPLIER** | A permanent power-up type: on catch, the player's current hit power is multiplied by 1.8 permanently for the rest of the run. Multiple catches stack multiplicatively (two catches = ×3.24 = 1.8 × 1.8). Reset only on Restart Game or a new run. **Exempt from the single-active-effect slot** (F11 AC5) — it has no timer, never blocks or is blocked by temporary effects, and is independent. Visible in the HUD. | PRD §F7 AC7, §F11 AC5, AC10 |
| **guaranteed drop** | One power-up per level is guaranteed to drop from a random enemy when it is destroyed. Additional drops beyond the guaranteed one occur at a 10% probability per enemy death (a tunable default, `EXTRA_DROP_CHANCE`). | PRD §F7 AC1 |
| **i-frames** | Short for "invulnerability frames." A brief post-hit invulnerability window (tunable, default 1.5 seconds) during which the player is immune to enemy laser damage and visibly distinguished (non-color-only). Not the same as the SHIELD power-up effect (which is a temporary active effect from F11's slot); this is automatic after any hit. | PRD §F8 AC9; NFR-9(a) |
| **Game Over** | Terminal state reached when the player loses: either lives reach 0 or the enemy formation reaches the player's row. Displays a clear Game Over message with an option to restart. If both loss conditions occur in the same frame, the game resolves to a single deterministic Game Over (not two competing screens). | PRD §F8 AC4, AC5, AC8 |
| **Game Complete** (celebration) | (v2) A 5-second celebratory end sequence that plays after defeating the **level-10 boss** (not after level-10 formation clear — the boss phase is required). Shows the text "Game Complete" on the black background with multi-colored firework explosions animating around it. After 5 seconds, automatically returns to the title screen. Pressing any key (except Esc) during the celebration **holds** the screen so you can read/screenshot the score; a second press advances to the title. Esc remains a no-op (F6 AC8 convention). Introduced in F19. | PRD §F19; `src/systems/VictoryCelebrationSystem.ts` |
| **level-intro countdown** | (v2) A 3-second "LEVEL [N]" freeze/fade that precedes active gameplay on every **fresh level start** (new run, level advance, or boss-to-next-level transition). During the countdown, the player and formation are visible but frozen — nobody moves, no firing, and input is wired but not live. The text fades as the countdown completes. **Restart Level is the sole exception** — it skips the intro for fast retry. Introduced in F18. | PRD §F18; `src/systems/LevelIntroSystem.ts` |
| **pause / PAUSED** | Game state entered by pressing Esc during active play. Freezes all motion (player, enemies, projectiles, power-ups, timers) and all effect durations. Pressing Esc again or selecting Resume continues exactly where the player left off with no loss of in-level progress. | PRD §F6; GameStateMachine |
| **screen state machine** | The explicit state machine managing the game's five screens: TITLE (start), PLAYING (active gameplay), PAUSED (pause overlay), GAMEOVER (lose state), and VICTORY (win state, now Game Complete celebration per F19). Input is routed per-screen so Esc means different things on different screens. | ADR-0002; GameStateMachine.ts |
| **fixed timestep** | A game-loop pattern where simulation advances in fixed-size time steps (1/60 second in this game) regardless of the real wall-clock frame rate. Decouples simulation from rendering, ensures determinism, and prevents frame-rate-dependent physics bugs. The accumulator loop in `GameLoop.ts` implements this. | ADR-0002; GameLoop.ts |
| **World** | The single mutable object holding all game state: player position/timers, enemy array, projectile array (shields with bounce state), power-up array, the single active-effect slot (F11), formation position/direction, lives, score, current level, screen state, boss phase (F12), level-intro counter (F18), boss-warning counter (F12), celebration counter (F19), and more. All systems read and mutate this object each tick. | `src/core/types.ts` World interface |
| **system** | A pure-ish function that updates a specific aspect of the World each tick (e.g., MovementSystem, FormationSystem, CollisionSystem, LevelIntroSystem, BossWarningSystem, VictoryCelebrationSystem). Systems execute in a fixed, deterministic order so complex multi-system interactions (like two loss conditions in one frame) resolve consistently. | architecture component responsibilities; ADR-0002 |
| **LevelConfig** | An immutable data structure (one row per level) encoding the 10-level progression: formation size (rows × cols), enemy HP mix, **boss HP** (v2: null for levels without a boss; 5× the toughest regular tier for levels 5 and 10), formation speed multiplier, fire-rate multiplier, and guaranteed power-up drops. Read by systems; never branched-on in code. | ADR-0003; `src/config/levelConfig.ts` |
| **formation-approach warning** | A visual danger cue that appears when the enemy formation's lowest living enemy is one row above the player's row (F3 AC6). Combines a pulsing border and a shape/text cue (non-color-only per NFR-9a). The instant loss rule (formation reaching the player's row) remains unchanged; this is a one-row-early cue only. **Distinct from the boss-incoming warning (F12 AC10 — different color family and solid flash vs. pulse).** | PRD §F3 AC6, §F12 AC10; Open Question Q7 |
| **HUD** | Heads-up display: the on-screen persistent UI showing the player's current score, lives remaining, current level, permanent-multiplier readout, **single active-temporary-effect indicator and timer** (v2, replacing three parallel indicators), and the one-line control hint. Rendered as DOM overlays over the canvas in a reserved band with guaranteed contrast (NFR-9b). | PRD §F10, §F8 AC1, §F7 AC10-11, §F11 AC6 (single indicator); NFR-9(b) |
| **NFR** | Non-Functional Requirement. PRD §NFR-1..NFR-10 define performance, compatibility, accessibility, and IP constraints that transcend individual features (e.g., "load ≤3s," "60 FPS," "no licensed IP"). | PRD §Non-Functional Requirements |
| **ADR** | Architecture Decision Record. Documents a major design decision, its context, the chosen option, alternatives considered, and consequences. The project has 5 ADRs covering stack/rendering, loop/determinism, level data, art/IP, and instrumentation. | `docs/architecture/adr/` |
| **traceability** | The practice of linking code, tests, and docs back to the upstream requirements that justify them. Every feature in the PRD traces to market goals/use cases; every ADR traces to PRD requirements; every test traces to acceptance criteria; every final doc references its sources. | `traceability-conventions` skill |
| **canvas (or Canvas 2D)** | HTML5 graphics API for drawing 2D shapes, text, and images directly to a raster surface. Used here for all game-field rendering (entities, projectiles, backgrounds, **shield trails**, **boss-incoming warning cues**, **level-intro text**, **celebration fireworks**) to achieve 60 FPS with minimal overhead. HUD overlays are DOM. | ADR-0001; CanvasRenderer.ts |
| **procedural art** | Art generated by code (draw functions) rather than external image files or raster sprites. All sprites in this game (shield, Vanguard, Sentinels, lasers, power-ups) are drawn procedurally as vector shapes (humanoid designs in v2 per F13, F14, F17), keeping asset load near-zero and making IP compliance auditable. | ADR-0004; `src/render/shapes.ts` |
| **instrumentation** | Lightweight telemetry emitted during play to track events like "session started," "level reached," "power-up caught," "run restarted." Stored in browser localStorage as anonymous integer counters; never sent to a server. Supports NFR-8 (observability of market goals B1–B4/P1–P5). | PRD §NFR-8; architecture §Data Flow; `src/instrumentation/Instrumentation.ts` |
