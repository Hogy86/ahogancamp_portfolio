# Voice of the Player — Captain America vs. Ultron: Shield Invaders

**Stage:** 1 — Marketing Analyst (pre-PRD)
**Date:** 2026-07-06
**Author:** marketing-analyst subagent
**Status:** Input to PRD. Do not treat as a spec — no features are prescribed here.

## 1. Framing

This is a browser-based, single-player 2D arcade shooter: a Space-Invaders-formula
game reskinned with a Captain America vs. Ultron theme. There is no enterprise
"customer" — the audience is a **player**, arriving through a browser, most
likely via a direct link, a portfolio/demo page, or a casual game portal
(itch.io, Newgrounds, CrazyGames, Poki-style aggregators). Session times are
short (minutes, not hours), intent is nostalgia/novelty-driven, and the
purchase decision is really an **attention decision**: does the player give
this tab 90 seconds, and do they come back.

"Voice of customer" here means: what makes a returning-genre player (or a
lapsed one) actually stay for a session and replay it, versus bouncing in the
first 15 seconds.

## 2. Player segments

### Segment A — Nostalgic arcade replayer
- **Who they are:** Played Space Invaders, Galaga, or similar formation
  shooters historically (arcade cabinets, emulators, or browser clones).
  Likely 30-55, browsing casually, often on a work break or a "let me kill 5
  minutes" impulse.
- **Core pain point:** Most free browser clones of Space Invaders are
  extremely literal — same sprites, same slow ramp, no twist — and feel stale
  within one play. They came for a quick nostalgia hit but find nothing new
  to hold attention past the first life.
- **Current workaround:** Bounce between near-identical clones
  (freeinvaders.org, classicgaming.cc, retrogames.cc ports) or give up and
  watch a YouTube longplay instead of playing.
- **What "better" looks like to them:** The familiar formation-shooter rhythm
  intact (so muscle memory transfers instantly), but with visible novelty —
  theme, power-ups, escalating stakes — that gives them a reason to finish a
  run rather than close the tab after level 1.

### Segment B — Marvel/MCU casual fan
- **Who they are:** Not necessarily a "gamer" — drawn in by the Captain
  America/Ultron premise itself (fan art, meme, social share, or a friend's
  link), not by genre loyalty. Wide age range, skews toward casual mobile/
  browser game habits rather than core gamer habits.
- **Core pain point:** Official Marvel games are console/PC titles (or
  paid/complex, e.g. Marvel Cosmic Invasion) or arcade-cabinet fighting
  games (Marvel Super Heroes) — nothing free, instant, and browser-based
  that lets them "be" Captain America for two minutes with zero install and
  zero learning curve. The fan-game space that does exist (ZVitor-style
  beat-em-ups, OpenBOR mods) requires downloads and setup that this audience
  won't tolerate.
- **Current workaround:** Watch clips, browse fan art, or play unrelated
  free superhero browser games (KBHGames-style tag pages) that don't
  scratch the specific "throw the shield" fantasy.
- **What "better" looks like to them:** Immediate legibility — no manual
  needed, the shield-throw *is* the hook — and a power fantasy payoff (the
  shield feels strong, Ultron robots feel satisfying to destroy) even if
  they've never played a formation shooter before.

### Segment C — Skill-chaser / completionist
- **Who they are:** Plays browser/indie arcade games specifically to beat
  them — clear all levels, find the "true" difficulty ceiling, screenshot a
  high score. Comfortable with keyboard-only controls and precise timing.
- **Core pain point:** Many free browser shooters either (a) never end —
  infinite waves with no defined "win" — or (b) are trivially short with no
  real difficulty curve, so there's nothing to master or brag about.
- **Current workaround:** Seeks out games with visible level counters or
  leaderboards; abandons games within the first level if difficulty is flat.
- **What "better" looks like to them:** A visible, finite goal (a stated max
  level), a legible and escalating difficulty curve they can feel level to
  level, and clear moments (two-hit enemies, boss patterns) that require
  actually improving play, not just repetition.

### Segment D — Time-boxed casual/break player
- **Who they are:** Opens the tab specifically because they have 3-10
  minutes (lunch break, waiting on something, killing time between
  meetings). Values instant load and instant restart above all else.
- **Core pain point:** Games that require account creation, long loading
  screens, tutorials, or unskippable intros burn the exact window this
  player has available, so they never get to the "fun" part before closing
  the tab.
- **Current workaround:** Sticks to the handful of games they already know
  load fast (e.g. browser Dino game, familiar clone sites) rather than
  risking an unknown link.
- **What "better" looks like to them:** Sub-second-feeling load, controls
  that are explained in one line (arrow keys, space, Esc) or not explained
  at all because they're self-evident, and a pause option (Esc) that
  respects the fact they may need to leave mid-session without losing
  progress or dignity.

## 3. Evidence from comparable/competitive research

### Direct competitors — Space Invaders clones (same problem, same approach)
- **freeinvaders.org, classicgaming.cc, retrogames.cc, ClassicReload.com** —
  faithful browser ports/clones of the 1978 original. Strength: instant
  legibility, zero learning curve, historically proven loop. Gap: visually
  and mechanically identical to every other clone; no theming, no power-up
  layer, no finite structure — nothing to differentiate one tab from
  another, so there's no reason to pick this one over the dozen others that
  rank alongside it in search results.
- **itch.io "space-invaders" tag** — indie developers frequently reskin the
  formula (different sprites/themes) as a learning project or jam entry.
  Strength: shows the formula tolerates reskinning well and that there's an
  active, receptive audience for genre variants. Gap: production quality and
  polish vary wildly; most are one-off prototypes without difficulty
  progression or a defined end state.

### Indirect competitors — licensed-IP / superhero arcade games
- **Marvel Super Heroes (Capcom, 1995, now browser-playable via
  emulation/archive sites)** — a licensed fighting game, not a shooter;
  shows there is durable demand for "play as this specific Marvel character"
  even decades later, but requires no interaction with the shooter genre.
- **MARVEL Cosmic Invasion (Tribute Games/Dotemu/Marvel Games, announced for
  2025)** — an official arcade-style beat-em-up on paid platforms
  (Switch/PlayStation/Xbox/Steam). Strength: proves Marvel is actively
  investing in nostalgia-arcade-styled titles right now, validating the
  "retro arcade + Marvel" positioning. Gap: it's a paid, installed,
  multi-character brawler — not a free, single-session, browser-instant
  experience. This project sits in the gap below it: no install, no
  purchase, one recognizable hero, one clear premise.
- **Fan games (ZVitor's Marvel Infinity War, OpenBOR mods, Justice League
  Legacy)** — community-made beat-em-ups with real production value.
  Strength: proves fans will build and play unofficial superhero games for
  free. Gap: distributed as downloadable mods/executables, not
  browser-instant; also brawler mechanics, not the low-friction
  arrow-keys-and-spacebar formula this game uses.
- **"Do nothing" (indirect, non-game competitor)** — for segment B and D
  especially, the real competitor is closing the tab and scrolling social
  media instead. This is the true baseline the game must beat: is it more
  compelling than 90 more seconds of the feed they were already in.

### Distribution/portal landscape (context for reach, not a feature ask)
- Aggregator portals (CrazyGames-scale ~35M MAU, Poki-scale ~60M MAU,
  Newgrounds/Armor Games for dedicated retro/indie audiences) are the
  discovery layer this genre lives or dies by. Portal curation criteria
  consistently reward: fast load, no login wall, clear controls, and a
  visible "hook" in the first few seconds of a thumbnail/preview — all
  directly relevant to how segments A-D behave, even if this specific build
  is not being submitted to a portal at launch.

## 4. Synthesis: what the market is telling us

1. The base formula (Space Invaders) is free, ubiquitous, and
   undifferentiated — the opportunity is not "build a shooter," it's "give a
   player a reason to pick this shooter over the ten identical ones already
   ranking in search."
2. The Marvel/Captain-America premise is the primary differentiator, but
   only if it's *instantly* accessible (no install, no account, no
   multi-character roster to learn) — that's the gap between this project
   and both the official Cosmic Invasion title and the fan-made beat-em-ups.
3. Finite, escalating structure (defined levels, a visible ceiling, a
   difficulty curve) is what separates a "toy" from a "game" for the
   skill-chaser segment and gives every segment a reason to keep playing
   past level 1.
4. Session-respecting design (fast load, one-line controls, a pause that
   actually pauses) is not a nice-to-have for this genre — it is the
   baseline entry price for the time-boxed casual segment, who will bounce
   immediately if it's violated.
